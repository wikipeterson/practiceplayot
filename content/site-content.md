### About PracticePlayOT
PracticePlayOT offers Occupational Therapy services to developing children.

Our therapy approach is rooted in fun, functional and purposeful engagement in activities and exercises for children (and support to families in their roles), in order to address deficits in their occupational roles as children, students, siblings, friends and community members.

Sessions with PracticePlay OT focus on the following:
- Printing skills
- Shoulder and core stability
- Postural alignment and pencil grip
- Spacial relations and personal space
- Strategies for behavioral organization and self-regulation
- Group skills (attending, following routines, taking turns, waiting)
- Following routines

Our staff consists of licensed, experienced Occupational Therapists.

## About Us

### What is an Occupational Therapist?
OTs help others develop skills for the job of living. A child's occupational role in life consists of being a student, family member, friend/peer and member of society. Many children could benefit from time with an OT to faciliate their occupational roles as their sensory and motor systems mature.

I worked for many years with agencies providing publicly-funded services to children in their roles in the classroom. I now have a private practice focused on working with children who may not qualify for such services but can still benefit from the support an occupational therapist can provide. Some of these children may have missed a cutoff for intervention by a point or two, while others are experiencing temporary challenges at a particular time in their development. These difficulties impede the childs' learning, friendships, and home life. I focus on teaching the child strategies, providing education to the family, and working on deficits. This approach has been a great way to help children become happier and more competent in their social and academic roles.

After a successful career in investment banking working in human resources, developent, and training in New York City and San Francisco, I returned to Philadelphia to pursue my passion for helping others. I attended Thomas Jefferson University and earned a Master's in Occupational Therapy with a focus on Sensory Integration in 2005. My career has consisted of serving as a Senior Level Therapist at the Hospital of the University of Pennsylvania with experience in the neonatal intensive care units and acute care teams as well as pediatric contract work through the Intermediate Unit, Early Intervention, and private consulting. I currently live in Haverford Township, have three active children and a wonderful husband who has a PhD in education.

## Services → Blossom Club

(Header graphic: `blossom-club.png`, 1400×921)

Blossom Club offers small group training for children ages 4-6 years. In this setting, opportunities are provided for children to develop and refine the skills necessary to participate successfully in academic, social, & play environments.

Sessions are 45 minutes long, and consist of engaging, creative, and guided play that addresses key areas of fine motor and speech and language development. Activities are structured to prepare children for independent and group activities that require self-regulation, attention and focus, successful peer interactions, conversational skills, fine motor skills, and visual/auditory processing. In addition, art, sensory, and science activities are explored in a fun and safe way.

We look forward to meeting you!

## Services → Staff In-service training

### A Sensory Approach to Learning
We are Occupational Therapists with a combined 20 years of experience with pediatrics in rehabilitation and early intervention settings. We offering staff training presentations designed to educate teachers about the benefits of emphasizing a sensory approach in the classroom. We provide a general overview of sensory processing, sensory dysregulation, and problematic behaviors commonly seen in children with sensory dysregulation. We present strategies that will benefit ALL students in the classroom. Teachers will learn how to implement a research based sensory approach to early childhood education for improved self-regulation, attention, participation, behavior and future academic success.
